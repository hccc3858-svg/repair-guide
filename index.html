Dorm Repairs & Electricity in China

A short, practical answer for international students who need help with a broken socket, light or air conditioner.

The question

“The socket, light, or air conditioner in my room is not working. How do I request a repair?”

🔧How to request a repair

Most Chinese universities let you report a fault through one of these three channels. Use whichever your school offers — the building front desk is the fastest for urgent problems.

1 · Building front desk / duty room （宿管 / 值班室）

Go to the front desk of your dormitory building (usually on the ground floor). Tell the staff your room number and what is broken. For emergencies — like a total power failure or a leak — go in person or call the duty number immediately.

2 · Online repair platform （网上报修）

Most schools have a repair service inside their official WeChat account, a mini-program or an app (look for “报修” = “repair request”). Fill in your building & room number, choose the type (electricity / light / air conditioner), describe the fault, add your contact and a photo if possible, then submit. You get a repair order and can track its progress.

3 · Repair hotline / apartment service centre （后勤报修热线）

Many campuses publish a repair or logistics hotline. International students can also ask the Overseas Students Office or the International Services Center for help.

📋What happens next

  1. You submit the request (in person, online, or by phone).
  2. The maintenance department reviews the order (接单) and assigns a worker.
  3. A worker comes to your room — routine faults are usually fixed within about 24–48 hours.
  4. You check the result and confirm (or rate) the repair when it is done.

⚡China's electricity — quick facts

  • Voltage: 220 V
  • Frequency: 50 Hz
  • Sockets / plugs: Type A, C and I — Type I (three flat pins in a “V”) is the most common.
⚠ Check your devices first. If you brought a charger or appliance from a 100–120 V country (e.g. the US or Japan), make sure it says “100–240 V” — most phone and laptop chargers do. If it only says “120 V”, you need a voltage converter; plugging it in directly can damage it.
💡 Before reporting a “broken” socket: try another device in the same socket, and check whether a circuit breaker in your room has tripped (push the switch back to ON). This often fixes the problem without waiting for a repair.
